SYNTHETIC MOCK DOCUMENT
This document contains fictional, synthetically created data. It is
not intended for clinical care, coding, billing, legal interpretation, or payer
submission.

1. PURPOSE
This synthetic guide describes how a software workflow should distinguish chart
evidence types. It is not an official coding manual and does not replace
qualified clinical or coding judgment.

2. CONFIRMED DIAGNOSES
A condition may be treated as documented only when a qualified source explicitly
states the diagnosis for the patient or otherwise clearly establishes it in the
assessment. The workflow should retain the source document, section, and wording
that supports the conclusion.

3. NON-DIAGNOSTIC INFORMATION
Symptoms, medication lists, laboratory results, imaging observations, and
problem-oriented discussions can provide context. They do not by themselves
establish an unstated diagnosis in this synthetic dataset. A reduced laboratory
value is not equivalent to a chronic disease diagnosis. A medication does not
prove the indication for which it was prescribed.

4. FAMILY HISTORY, NEGATION, AND UNCERTAINTY
Family history belongs to a relative unless the source separately diagnoses the
patient. Statements such as "no COPD," "denies diabetes," or "not present" must
remain connected to their negation. Terms such as "possible," "rule out," or
"under evaluation" should not be converted to confirmed disease.

5. SOURCE-BOUND OUTPUT
A generated response should say that evidence is insufficient when the retrieved
sources do not support the requested fact. It must not fill a gap with general
medical knowledge. A code lookup reference can display an entry, but it cannot
create evidence that the condition applies to the patient.
