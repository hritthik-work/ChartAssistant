CHUNKING STRATEGY - QUICK DEV LOG

At first I thought I could split every file after a fixed number of characters. That was simple, but
it could separate a condition from its heading, date, or status. In a patient chart, that small bit
of context matters a lot.

I then tried smaller and larger chunks while checking the sample questions. Very small chunks were
good for exact matches, but they often lost the full meaning of a section. Bigger chunks kept more
context, but they also brought in extra text and made the citations less focused.

The middle ground that worked best for this dataset was:

- target size: 180 tokens
- maximum size: 240 tokens
- overlap: 30 tokens
- total context budget: 900 tokens
- chunk ID: <document-id>-chunk-<order>

I split by document section first, not only by size. This keeps headings such as ACTIVE CONDITIONS,
FAMILY HISTORY, or MEDICATIONS attached to the right text. If a section is too long, it is split at
sentence boundaries. The 30-token overlap keeps the next chunk from starting with no context.

I also add the title, section name, and source type to the embedding input. The original chart text
is still kept as it is, which is useful when the app needs to show an exact quote.

These numbers are not meant to be perfect for every medical document. They are a practical middle
ground for the short synthetic charts in this project. If this had thousands of long charts, I would
test the sizes again on real evaluation questions and add document-level filtering before searching
individual chunks.
